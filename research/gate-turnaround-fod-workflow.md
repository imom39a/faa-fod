## Key findings

### 1. Binding layer: no federal rule prescribes a gate FOD workflow

- **FAA says so explicitly.** "There is no Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR) rule that specifically addresses aircraft ground handling, servicing, and marshalling; however, elements of these topics could be addressed under ... part 121, §§ 121.133, 121.135, and 121.137 ... and part 139, § 139.329." (AC 00-34B ¶1.)
- **Part 121 (binds the airline).** The airline must keep "a manual for the use and guidance of flight, **ground operations**, and management personnel" (§121.133(a)). The manual must contain "instructions and information necessary to allow the personnel concerned to perform their duties ... with a high degree of safety" (§121.135(a)(1)) and the "Duties and responsibilities of ... **appropriate members of the ground organization**" (§121.135(b)(2)). Servicing and refuelling procedures are also required (§121.135(b)(17), (19)). **Nothing in Part 121 names FOD, walks, the ERA or pushback.** An airline's gate FOD procedure is binding on its staff because it is in the airline's own manual, not because the FAA prescribes its content.
- **Part 5 SMS (binds the airline).** Part 5 applies to every Part 121 certificate holder (§5.1(a)). It requires safety performance monitoring, including "monitoring of operational processes" and a confidential hazard-reporting system (§5.71(a)(1), (7)). It sets retention: safety-assurance outputs **≥5 years**, risk-management outputs "as long as the control remains relevant" (§5.97(a)–(b)). This is the regulatory hook for FOD Findings and Confirmations if the airline treats them as safety-assurance data. Whether it does is an airline decision.
- **Part 139 (binds the airport).** §139.329 requires the airport to limit and control pedestrians and ground vehicles in **movement and safety areas**, and to train people annually. It requires training records kept 24 months after access ends, and movement-area accident or incident records kept 12 months (§139.329(f)). It does **not** cover the apron or gate (a non-movement area). §139.305(a)(4) (FOD removal on "loading ramp, and parking area") is in the earlier research.
- **OSHA (binds the employer of ramp staff).** Under the 2000 MOU, for "maintenance personnel and ground support personnel", "OSHA has been enforcing, and will continue to enforce, OSHA requirements ... to the extent allowed under Section 4(b)(1)". 29 CFR 1910.22 requires walking-working surfaces to be "maintained free of hazards such as sharp or protruding objects ... spills, snow, and ice" (a)(3), "inspected, regularly and as necessary" (d)(1), and hazards "corrected or repaired before an employee uses the walking-working surface again" (d)(2). *Inference, not verified against an OSHA interpretation:* the ramp a worker walks on is a walking-working surface, so 1910.22 gives a worker-safety basis for removing debris. It sets no gate timing. Preemption under §4(b)(1) applies where FAA-accepted manuals govern a condition, so the boundary is case by case.
- **FAA advisory, addressed to air carriers (not binding).** AC 00-65A applies to "all operators under ... parts 91, 91K, 121, 125, 129, and 135" and their agents (¶1). SAFO 23006 asks the same operators to review towing and marshalling procedures through their SMS. Neither mentions FOD walks. Both are about personnel safety during aircraft movement.

### 2. FAA ground-handling guidance relevant to the gate workflow (new)

- **Engine ingestion zone:** "Ground personnel who are not performing an authorized and coordinated maintenance check should not enter a turbine engine ingestion zone ... until the engines are shut down." (AC 00-34B ¶11.) SAFO 23006 action 4: "all personnel to remain clear of operating engines until they are shut down". Its trigger case was a fatal ingestion when a ramp agent went to the forward cargo door with the #1 engine still running for cool-down. During gate pushback the danger zone is managed by the carrier's procedure: personnel must "avoid rotating propellers, the danger zones of jet engines" (AC 00-65A ¶11.21; AC 00-34B ¶13.2.8, which notes pushback with engines running is "an exception" to the no-engines-while-towing rule).
- **Beacon cue:** "Before an aircraft engine is started, pilots are supposed to turn on the anti-collision beacon(s) ... However, don't assume that if the beacon(s) aren't flashing that the engine(s) isn't (aren't) running." (AC 5210-20A App. B §2.2.3. App. B is a *sample* airport training manual, not a rule.)
- **Zone size (737NG, manufacturer data):** inlet hazard radius 10 ft (3.1 m) with 4 ft (1.2 m) aft distance at idle, and 14 ft (4.2 m) / 5 ft (1.5 m) at breakaway and takeoff thrust (Boeing D6-58325-7 Rev C §6.1.28). FAA gives no dimensions.
- **Chocks before activity:** procedures should require "that activities commence only after chocks are installed" and "all personnel to remain clear of the aircraft in tow until the aircraft has come to a complete stop" (SAFO 23006 actions 2–3; AC 00-65A ¶11.3).
- **Clear the vicinity before movement:** "The CH should remove and store all equipment, work stands, loose aircraft parts, and other materials from the vicinity of an aircraft." (AC 00-65A ¶11.18.) Close doors and retract ladders (¶11.19). Tiedown items "can create trip and foreign object damage (FOD) hazards if not properly accounted for" (AC 00-34B ¶12.1).
- **Walk-around before engine start:** AC 00-65A App. A, *Checklist Example*, heads its first block "**(Prior to engine start, perform the following visual walk-around safety checks)**". The items are tyres, fluid leaks, structural damage, hydraulic quantity, "three (3) sets of chocks", and bypass pins. This is an **aircraft-condition** walk-around, not a FOD sweep, and it is an *example* checklist.
- **Roles:** a wing walker "should be stationed at each wingtip" and signals the tug driver to stop (AC 00-65A ¶11.9). A tail walker is used for sharp turns or backing (¶11.10). The marshaller relies on wing and tail walkers (¶11.8). The marshaller must be "trained, qualified, and approved" (AC 00-34B ¶14.3). "Emergency stop instructions from any team member should be obeyed" (AC 00-65A ¶11.7). The tug operator stops on loss of communication with the cockpit observer, tower or ramp control (¶11.7). Ground personnel should be briefed on the communications in use (hand signals, radios) "before movement of aircraft" (¶11.4). AC 00-34B Figure 3 shows "One Marshaller and a Guide During a Gate Pushback Operation with a Tug". **FAA guidance never uses "headset operator" or "pushback lead"** and gives no one authority over engine-start clearance.
- **Overwatch:** "When an individual is conducting a highly focused task on or around an aircraft ... there should be another individual providing overwatch" (AC 00-34B ¶11).
- **Post-park visual check (aircraft condition):** "make a visual check of the aircraft as soon as it is parked and secured" and report unsafe conditions to the flight crew (AC 00-34B ¶12.3).

### 3. GSE staging and the equipment restraint line: FAA defers to industry

- **FAA has no marking standard for gate stands.** "Surface markings for large airplane parking positions ... will be addressed at a future date in additional chapters." (AC 5340-1M ¶1.)
- AC 5300-13B §5.12.2 says outright that safety envelopes, equipment parking and engine hazard zones are industry practice: "Industry practices and guidelines are available for select apron markings including **aircraft safety envelopes**, passenger walk paths, **equipment parking**, passenger-board-bridge area, and **engine hazard zones**. Individual airlines may establish company standards for gate area markings at gates the airline contractually controls." It points to ACI *Apron Markings and Signs* and A4A *Recommended Apron Markings and Identifications*. Figure 5-2 labels a "Safety envelope", "GSE Area" and "Vehicle Limit Line" around a parking position. §5.6.2 recommends a vehicle limit line to keep vehicles and equipment clear of the taxilane object-free area.
- **IGOM 4.1.1(d)** (quoted in IATA Gap-1/Gap-2): "Make sure all required ground support equipment (GSE), chocks and safety cones are available and serviceable, and are positioned well clear of the aircraft path, **outside the equipment restraint area (ERA)**." **IGOM 3.1.3.4**: "Make one complete stop with all motorized GSE prior to entering the ERA or at 5 m from the aircraft. This action shall be carried out **even if there is no equipment restraint line marked** on the apron." **IGOM 3.1.3.2(c)**: "Check that all areas of GSE are free of contamination, FOD and safe for use prior to and throughout the operation."
- Worked example (Changi GOSM §5.2.1.1): "The ERA is defined as the area of the apron bordered by a red line. It shall be kept clear at all times for the safe movement of an aircraft in and out of the stand." GSE may enter the ERA only after a complete stop, engines off and spooling down, anti-collision lights off, chocks in, grounding connected, and the headset man's thumbs-up (§5.3.2.1, §5.6.1.2).
- FAA sample airport rules (AC 5210-20A App. B ¶q, s, t): apron equipment "may be parked only in approved areas"; no parking "in an aircraft parking area". ATL enforces staging as violations: C1.4 "Unauthorized parked vehicle / equipment", C2.4 and C3.14 "Staging vehicles or equipment in unauthorized areas" (ATL NOVRSS 2024).

### 4. FOD walks in IGOM and airport practice

- **IGOM 4.1.1 Actions Prior to Aircraft Arrival** (verbatim in IATA Gap-2 §2.2.1): "(a) Ensure all persons involved with the aircraft arrival and post-arrival handling/servicing are briefed on safety and operational requirements ... (b) **Conduct a foreign object debris (FOD) check of the entire stand, removing all debris just prior to aircraft arrival.** (c) Make sure the stand surface condition is sufficiently free of ice, snow, etc. ... (d) [GSE outside ERA, above] (e) Make sure the aircraft guidance docking system is activated, where applicable, or a marshaller is in position ... (f) Make sure required ground personnel are present including any additional personnel (i.e., wing walker), if applicable."
- IATA treats a company manual that moves the FOD check to **after aircraft departure** as a *variation* from IGOM 4.1.1(b) that needs an SMS risk assessment (Gap-2 §5.2.1, §4.4.2). So post-departure-only is a known non-conforming practice.
- **IGOM Chapter 4 structure** (Gap-1 §3, section-level example): 4.1 Aircraft Arrival, 4.2 Chocking, 4.3 Coning, 4.4 Access Doors, 4.5 Loading and Unloading, 4.6 Aircraft Departure, 4.7 Open Ramp Departure, 4.8 Powerback, 4.9 Towing, 4.10 Long-Term Parking. **The text of IGOM 4.6 (pre-departure walk-around or FOD check) was not available from a first-party source.** Secondary copies (Scribd, older editions) say IGOM has a pre-departure walk-around and stand FOD walk. Treat that as unverified until the IGOM itself is read.
- **Worked example, pre-departure (Changi GOSM):** §5.7.6.1: "The RP shall walk around the entire aircraft to ensure that there is no equipment, spillage, FOD and personnel within the ERA". §5.7.6.2: the pushback tyre path must be free of FOD. §5.7.4.1: the responsible party for arrival is the docking-system operator or marshaller ("Arrival OIC"), and for departure it is the **headset man** ("Departure OIC"). §6.4.1: "The headset operator is recognized as the overall-in-charge for the departure phase ... empowered to point out any violation ... and demand for violation to be corrected." Pre-arrival walk path: the ERA boundary plus the stand centreline. Pause if an adjacent stand has an aircraft moving (§5.7.5.3–5.7.5.5).
- **Trade press (secondary):** one US ground handler's gate procedure walks the gate 5–10 minutes before arrival, plus post-flight and pre-push walks (AviationPros, "The FOD Squad"). No FAA or IATA timing number exists.

### 5. Hand-offs and escalation outside the gate

- The FAA leaves the airline/airport cleaning split to agreements (-24A §3.3.1; -18D §3.3). It offers designating handling-agent staff to inspect "prior to aircraft movement to and from the gate" (-24A §4.3.2.3.1, permissive).
- **ATL:** "While it is the responsibility of all airport personnel to refrain from creating FOD, **removing FOD from the movement area is the responsibility of Airport Operations**. While operating in the movement area, if FOD is spotted, do not attempt to maneuver, or chase, the debris. **Report all FOD to the ATCT or Airport Operations.**" (ATL AMA v5 p.22.) Airport Operations: 404-787-6095 (p.22). ATL has ramp-control frequencies Ramp 1–6, to be contacted "prior to pushback or entry into the ramp" when repositioning aircraft (p.15). Failure to report an accident, incident or hazmat spill to the Airport Operations Center is violation C2.2 (NOVRSS).
- **Correction to FOD-R10 / GLOSSARY:** the airport's stated FOD role at ATL is the **movement area**. Apron taxilanes behind concourse gates are generally **non-movement area** (AC 5210-20A App. B §2.1, "Non-movement areas include aprons ..."), usually under ramp control. Who removes FOD on an apron taxilane or adjacent stand is set by lease or LOA, and no public ATL source says. So "outside the gate safety envelope ⇒ airport" is too strong. It should be "outside the envelope ⇒ escalate per the station's LOA: ramp control and/or Airport Operations; movement area ⇒ ATCT or Airport Operations".
- Changi (worked example): FOD safety risks are reported to the Airside Management Centre (§5.7.2.3). The departure OIC calls AMC if operators don't comply (§6.4.1). Spills in the ERA go to AMC (§5.7.5 note).

### 6. Wing walkers at ATL

- ATL violation **C1.7: "Failure to provide 2 wing walkers during aircraft pushback or arrival"**. C2.1: failure to yield to wing walkers. C2.6 and C2.17: above-idle run-up or engine run-up testing on ramps without wing walkers. C3.4: "Driving between aircraft and marshaller". C1.3, C2.8 and C3.13: failure to secure trash, cargo, mail, or "FOD-producing items" (escalating class if it "becomes safety hazard/incur delays") (ATL NOVRSS 2024). These are **binding at ATL** through the airport's rules and City ordinance (§22-191), enforced on tenants and badge holders.

## Where the sources are silent (decisions for 0016)

1. **No FAA rule or AC requires a per-turn FOD record.** Records are required for FOD collected (-24A §6.1.1, advisory), SMS outputs (§5.97, if the airline treats Findings as SMS data), and training (§139.329(f), AC 00-34B ¶14.3). A per-Inspection record is a PoC choice. Keeping it ≥2 years matches -24A §6.1.4. Keeping it ≥5 years matches §5.97(b) if classed as safety assurance.
2. **No FAA or IATA minute value** for "just prior to arrival".
3. **Who may clear a HOLD** is an airline rule. FAA guidance only says any team member's emergency stop should be obeyed.
4. **Equipment restraint line geometry and colour** are not FAA-standardised. Red is common (Changi). A4A SG-908 and ACI handbook not read. Gate markings at a carrier-controlled gate may be the carrier's own standard (AC 5300-13B §5.12.2), so mapping the ERL per Gate is needed.
5. **GLOSSARY "Turn"** runs "from arrival (block-in) to pushback", but the pre-arrival Inspection happens before block-in. 0016 may want Turn to start at stand preparation.
