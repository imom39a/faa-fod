## 1. Concourse T: gate numbers and layout

**Location and orientation (AD, rotated to north-up).** The Domestic Terminal sits at the west end of the Central Passenger Terminal Complex (CPTC). Concourse T is the north-south pier on the terminal's east face. **Ramp 1** lies between Concourse T and Concourse A. The ramps run west to east: Ramp 1 (T/A), Ramp 2 (A/B), and on through Ramp 6. Ramp 1 is bounded by Twy E/F to the north and Twy L/M to the south. ATL ramps are named "North" and "South" by the end you are at (AMA p.3).

**Which side faces what.** The concourse's west side is the terminal building. Its gates face **east onto Ramp 1**, the taxilane shared with Concourse A's west-side gates.
- The T-map draws every gate along one edge only, labelled "GATES" (east on a north-left map, judging by the inset).
- The Commons aerial "ATL Terminal T and A 2018" (looking south) shows nose-in stands on both sides of Ramp 1.
- Ramp 1 taxilanes are **non-movement area**, not controlled by ATCT (AMA pp.4-5).
- The movement area starts at the red-and-white "zipper" marking, which is unique to ATL (AMA p.5; RSSM §IX.A).

**Gate numbering.**
- **T-map (rev Dec 2013, before the extension).** Labels run T1 (south end) to T15 (north end).
  - South half: T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T7, T8. No T6 label is printed. Wikipedia [secondary] mentions a Sky Club "near T6", so the gap is probably a missing label, not a missing gate.
  - North half: T9 to T15.
  - The two halves split at the central atrium and escalators to the Plane Train.
  - Clubs: Delta Sky Club markers on the south half, the American Admirals Club near T12/T13.
- **T-North extension (T-North PR, Dec 2022).** "new five Aircraft Design Group III gates, **T17-T21**, have the capacity for aircraft with a wingspan between 79 and 118 feet. These gates will primarily be used by United Airlines." It also added apron pavement with hydrant fueling.
  - Corgan [secondary] puts it differently: T16-T17 (undersized) relocated into the extension, plus four new ADG III gates T18-T21, with United on T16-T21.
- **Rates-26** lists "Concourse T-North Existing 4 Gates Modernization (T12-T15)", which confirms those numbers are the T-North block.
- **Gate count.** Wikipedia [secondary] says 21 gates. The numbering T1-T21 fits that, but no primary source lists all 21 stands.

## 2. Who uses which gates

- **Delta.** The best primary evidence is **DL-2017**, a Delta test at "T1, T2 and T3 gates" on the T Concourse, plus the Sky Club markers on the T-map. Wikipedia [secondary]: "The south side of Concourse T and all of Concourses A and B are used exclusively by Delta for mainline domestic flights."
- **American.** Lease §5.03.B(2)(a) (p.34): when the Concourse T Expansion opens, "three (3) additional Concourse T Gates will be assigned to American" on a Preferential Use basis, in exchange for three Concourse D gates. Corgan [secondary] says these are T13-T15.
- **United.** Lease §5.03.B(2)(b): "two (2) additional Concourse T Gates will be assigned to United". The T-North PR says T17-T21 are "primarily used by United".
- **Common use.** The lease requires the City to have at least three Domestic Common Use Gates after the Concourse T Expansion (definition of "City Common Use Gate Requirement").
- **Gap.** No public source gives a gate-by-gate assignment for today. The lease's Exhibit K is not public. **Treat "Delta = T1-T8 (south half)" as likely but unverified.** Confirm it with the Delta ATL station.

## 3. Typical narrowbody types

From DL-fleet (delta.com aircraft pages), Delta's mainline narrowbody types are A220-100, A220-300, A319, A320, A321, A321neo, 717-200, 737-800, 737-900ER, 757-200 and 757-300. Published wingspans:

| Type | Wingspan (delta.com) | ADG (by the T-North PR's 79-118 ft band) |
|---|---|---|
| A220-100 / -300 | 115 ft 1 in | III |
| 717-200 | 93 ft 4 in | III |
| 737-800 | 117 ft 5 in | III |
| 737-900ER | 117 ft 10 in | III |
| 757-200 (winglets) | 134 ft 9 in | **IV**, so it **cannot** use the T17-T21 ADG III gates |

delta.com's A319/A320/A321 pages did not load. The A320 family is ADG III (wingspan under 118 ft); that is general knowledge, not a sourced figure here.

**PoC implication:** the 0005 Resolution covers "A320 family / 737". At ATL that means A321/A321neo, 737-900ER and A220 are the common stand-fillers. The 757 is larger than ADG III, so its engine, wing and AFT Zones sit further out on any stand that takes it. Whether the T-south stands take 757s is **not public**.

## 4. Ramp control at ATL

- **Two layers.** The movement area belongs to ATCT. The non-movement area, meaning ramps, aprons, NLVR and taxilanes, "is not controlled by the air traffic control tower" (AMA pp.4-5).
- **Ramp control frequencies.** Aircraft repositioning on a terminal ramp must call Ramp Control first (AMA p.15: Ramp 1 131.45, Ramp 2 131.85, …).
- **Published frequencies.** CS p.206, "RAMP CTL": Ramp 1 131.45, Ramp 2 131.85, Ramp 3 129.275, Ramp 4 130.075, Ramp 5 129.375, Ramp 6 and Non Movement Area 131.375, Ramp 8 128.975, Ramp 9 131.875, plus snow-and-ice frequencies. The AD shows the same list. The 5010 record's frequency table matches, but its older free-text remark still gives Ramp 4 as 129.37 and Ramp 5 as 131.25, which is stale.
- **Who operates the towers. Partly verified, and not "all Delta".**
  - No FAA or City document names the tower operators.
  - **TBI Airport Management** (the ATL International Terminal and common-use manager, per Props) staffs **Ramps 3 and 4**. Its posting says the job is to "coordinate the aircraft traffic on ramps 3 and 4" and to act "as an extension of the City of Atlanta, Department of Aviation and ATL partner airlines" (TBI job [secondary, but it is the employer's own posting]).
  - Delta running **Ramp 1 (Concourse T/A) and Ramp 2 from the Concourse A ramp tower** is reported only in old forum posts (a.net [secondary]). Nothing current and first-party confirms it. **Treat "Delta Ramp Control owns Ramp 1" as probable; verify with Delta ATL.**
- The **FAA Airport Operations / DOA Ops** line for movement-area FOD is separate: 404-530-6620 (RSSM §IX.J).

## 5. Published ramp and FOD rules, and the responsibility split

### Airline / tenant
- **Lease §12.01.D (p.74):** "Airline shall keep, at its own expense, the **Aircraft Parking Area** free of fuel, oil, debris and other foreign objects resulting from Airline's use thereof."
- **Lease §12.01.B (p.71):** the Signatory Airlines collectively "shall provide operations and maintenance services for the Aircraft Parking Area and the **Aircraft Apron Taxilane Area**". They do this through AATC, the Atlanta Airlines Terminal Corporation, the airlines' third-party O&M provider (definition, p.5).
- **Lease definitions.**
  - "Aircraft Parking Area" is "the location where an aircraft parks at a Gate within the CPTC".
  - "Aircraft Apron Taxilane Area" is "the paved areas generally between the Aircraft Parking Area[s]".
  - "Gate" includes "the aircraft parking position, apron areas for staging ground service equipment, loading bridge, ground power units, …".
- **RSSM §IX.J (Litter/FOD):**
  - "It is the responsibility of all drivers to pick up FOD on the AOA ramps and roadways. Generating or knowingly failing to remove FOD may result in administrative action."
  - Drivers must check vehicle body, wheels and tires for FOD before entering the AOA.
  - Loose baggage and articles must be moved off taxilanes to the gate/building side.
  - No uncovered trash containers.
- **RSSM §VI:** "Tenants, stakeholders and contractors must proactively inspect work areas to ensure that they are free from hazardous conditions."

### Airport (City of Atlanta DOA)
- **AMA p.20:** "removing FOD from the **movement area** is the responsibility of Airport Operations … Report all FOD to the ATCT or Airport Operations."
- **RSSM §IX.J:** "If FOD is observed past the zipper markings on the taxiways or runways, the vehicle operator shall immediately contact the Airport Operations Divisions office at 404-530-6620. Do not cross the Zipper marking."
- **RSSM §VI:**
  - DOA oversees the Ramp Inspection Program.
  - "Airport Operations conducts clean sweep inspections for general cleanliness standards, FOD and wildlife attractant issues", with records kept for more than 24 months.
  - Hazards are logged in ASOCS (the Airport Security Operations Compliance System).
  - Responsible stakeholders must respond in writing every 30 days until the hazard is resolved.
  - Atlanta Fire Rescue (AFRD) holds "ramp managers accountable" for combustible debris.
- **FOD Walk PR:** "FOD inspections occur throughout the day to keep ATL runways clear of debris", conducted by ATL airside operations.
- **Enforcement.** NOVs under Atlanta City Code Ch. 22 (NOV page). FOD-related classes on NOV-24:
  - C1.3 / C2.8 / C3.12: failure to secure trash, cargo, mail or FOD-producing items.
  - C1.12: littering.
  - C2.4 / C3.13: staging vehicles or equipment in unauthorized areas.
  - Sanctions run from 1 to 3 points, with 2-day or 7-day loss of driving privileges.

### GSE staging and restraint
- **Equipment restraint lines:** **not found** in any public ATL document. RSSM, AMA, NOV-24 and the lease were all searched for "restraint" and "envelope", with zero hits for ERL or "safety envelope". The Glossary's ERL and Gate safety envelope come from airline/IGOM practice, not ATL rules (see ticket 0014).
- **RSSM §IX.C parking rules:**
  - No parking "under any passenger loading bridge".
  - No parking that will "interfere with or prevent the passage or movement of aircraft".
  - Nothing parked "within 10 feet of the security perimeter fence".
  - Vehicles blocking an aircraft or gate are reported to the AATC Communication Center (404-530-2112).
- **RSSM §IX.K:** "All tugs, trailers, baggage carts or cargo carts must be returned to their assigned storage areas immediately after unloading." Carts must be enclosed or secured on all four sides. Towing is limited to 5 carts; the 2024 poster cites "Towing more than 4 carts" (C1.1), so the newer limit is lower.
- **RSSM §IX.I:** no pushback, power-back or guided arrival "unless at least two Wing Walkers are deployed".
- The RSSM on atl.com is now a dead "Download" button (Props), even though "All tenants … are required to remain in full compliance of the most recent version". **No newer edition is published.**

### What this means for the Glossary split
The Glossary rule "inside envelope = airline, outside = Airport Ops" is **too simple for ATL**:
- The **Aircraft Parking Area** is the individual airline's.
- The **Apron Taxilane Area** (Ramp 1 between stands) is the Signatory Airlines' collective O&M through AATC, and ramp FOD is every driver's duty.
- **Airport Operations** owns FOD in the **movement area**, past the zipper marking.

For the PoC, "outside the stand but on Ramp 1" should route to the airline's ramp control or AATC. Only "beyond the zipper" goes to Airport Operations (404-530-6620). Confirm the exact routing with Delta ATL.

## 6. Recommended 5-Gate example set

These are **provisional**. All five are on the south half (the probable Delta side). Stand sizes, which stands take 757s, and camera positions are not public.

| Gate | Role in the set | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | End of pier | The southern-most stand. It is the first and last stand on Ramp 1's south end, closest to the Twy L/M side, with the most through-traffic and jet-blast exposure from aircraft entering or leaving the ramp. Delta-confirmed in DL-2017. |
| **T2** | End-neighbour pair with T1 | Tests the shared stand boundary: who owns FOD between T1 and T2, and how to de-duplicate a Finding seen by both Gates' WIDE Angles. Also tests a pre-departure Inspection while the neighbour is pushing. Delta-confirmed in DL-2017. |
| **T4** | Mid-concourse baseline | A stand with Delta neighbours on both sides and a typical taxilane exposure. This is the control stand. |
| **T5** | Mid-concourse, next to the T5/T7 gap | The T-map shows a non-gate stretch (no T6 label) between T5 and T7. That likely means irregular stand spacing or a nearby building feature, which makes it useful as an odd-geometry case for Angle-to-camera mapping. If T6 is a real stand, swap in T6. |
| **T8** | Inner end, next to the centre atrium and the north half | The south half's inboard end. Its neighbour (T9 onward) is probably another carrier or common use. This tests the case where FOD drifts in from a stand run under a different airline's process. |

Not chosen: **T17-T21** (ADG III, mostly United) and **T13-T15** (American).

**Alternative if variety matters more than Delta purity:** swap T5 for **T21**, the last T-North stand at the north end, to get a second end-of-pier stand with ADG III limits. It is a United gate, so it only works if the example is "Concourse T", not "Delta Concourse T".

**Open discrepancy:** 0005's Resolution says "One shared set for all **6** Gates", while the Glossary and the brief say **5**. Fix it in 0005 or the Glossary.
