## Question

From FAA AC 150/5210-24 and the linked FAA FOD pages, extract: FOD definitions and categories (by material/source), any severity or risk classification, inspection practices for gates/aprons (frequency, FOD walks, timing vs pushback), required responses (removal, reporting, stopping operations), and record-keeping expectations. Note where the AC is silent so the airline/PoC must decide. Cite section numbers.

## Resolution

Findings: [research/faa-fod-guidance.md](../research/faa-fod-guidance.md).
- Cite **AC 150/5210-24A** (2024); the original -24 is cancelled.
- The binding rule is 14 CFR §139.305(a)(4), which applies to the airport: debris on ramps and parking areas must be "removed promptly". §139.327 requires daily inspections and keeping records for 12 months.
- The FAA gives FOD sources (§2.2.2.1), a list of typical FOD types (§2.2.3.1) and gate hotspots (§3.3.1, §4.3.1.1.3), but **no severity scale**. The only distinction is binary: an immediate hazard can stop operations; anything else is removed when the schedule permits (§4.1.1.1).
- Gate inspection timing is permissive only (§4.3.2.3.1). Record fields are listed in §6.1.1; keep records 2 years (§6.1.4).
- Left for the airline to decide: severity levels, whether a Finding blocks pushback and who can stop an operation, removal deadline, category codes, retention. There's no FAA guidance on AI detection; the closest benchmarks are in AC 150/5220-24.
